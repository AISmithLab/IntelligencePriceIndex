## Abstract

We introduce the Intelligence Price Index (IPI), a quarterly matched-model price index for cognitive-labor services, and use it to ask how much the price of that labor has moved during the generative AI era. The index is built from **37,782 archived Fiverr gig-price snapshots** retrieved from the Internet Archive — a 500-seller longitudinal pilot plus a dense trailing crawl — across **seven service categories**. Captures reach back to 2018 and the estimation panel opens in 2019Q4, but we **publish the index only from 2020Q1 to 2026Q1**: four quarters of 2017–2018 contain no captures at all, and §3.7 shows the 2018Q3–2020Q1 leg is not identified, its level moving by a third under a change of base quarter alone. The index is estimated with GEKS-Jevons, a multilateral estimator that holds each gig fixed against itself and eliminates the chain drift that irregular archival sampling would otherwise induce.

The composite index rises **+40.7% in real terms** (+78.4% nominal) against CPI-U of +26.8%, with a 95% band of ±3.7%. We then measure the leading rivals to an AI account of that rise rather than arguing them away. General inflation accounts for roughly half of the nominal increase. Reputation accumulation accounts for a large further share: on a panel of **169,337 gig-quarters across 15,676 gigs**, price within a gig rises **+7.33% per doubling of cumulative reviews** in real terms ($t = 25.9$), replicating a pilot estimate on fifty times the data; rebuilding the index on reputation-adjusted prices gives a composite band of **+39.7% to +79.0%** whose floor is itself imprecise.

**On two independent price objects we find no AI effect, and we report both as silences rather than zeros.** The pre-registered exposure difference-in-differences returns `Exposure × Post` = **−0.0333** ($t$ −1.35) — an estimate *below* its own minimum detectable effect of 0.0689, which fails parallel trends and reverses sign against a linear trend. Extending the panel four quarters into recovered 2025 data moves it to −0.0324. Separately, we recover **681,668 realised orders** from archived pages, dated by order rather than capture, and show that the apparent collapse in realised value they seem to display — orders under \$50 rising from ~0% to ~54% of the market — is **entirely a reporting change**, since sub-\$50 orders carried no price band at all before 2024Q2. On the bands that are stable across every era, the median realised order sits in **\$100–200 in all seventeen quarters**.

The negative results are part of the contribution. Six of seven categories miss the ±5% precision standard we state in advance, so no category ranking is supported. At a finer 35-category cut, the two most AI-exposed cells — translation and subtitling — are the only two whose real price change covers zero, but neither breaks at ChatGPT's release and the largest bucket in the same family rises +51.2%. A benchmark-linked "price elasticity of intelligence," which earlier versions of this work reported, is a **spurious regression** and is retracted; a market-measured exposure variable that appears to give +13.76% per 10pp collapses to +0.73% against category-specific trends.

Finally, we document that **the archival record closed during the study**. Internet Archive 403 rates on this domain rise from 1.0% in 2025Q4 to 89.3% in 2026Q2, adjacent-quarter revisit rates fall from 74–82% to 34.5%, and Common Crawl never covered the site at all. The window shut at roughly the moment the question became answerable. We contribute a replicable instrument, honest bounds on what pilot-scale archival data can resolve, and the prospective collection design that a successor study would need — because the retrospective one is no longer available.
