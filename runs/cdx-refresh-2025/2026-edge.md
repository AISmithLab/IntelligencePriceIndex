# Is there any 2026 Fiverr data left to collect?

**Run:** 2026-09-07, ~23:00-23:10 UTC. Three sources tested, cheaply, before committing
to any large pull. Two are exhausted; the third is the only one that reaches 2026Q2-Q3.

## 1. Wayback: the crawler kept going and the wall took it

**CORRECTED 2026-09-07, later the same evening.** The first version of this note read
the quarter distribution off prefix `z` alone — half of one small prefix, 1,465 records —
and concluded that "the captures simply stop" and that "403s do not rise". Both are
wrong, and the operator was right to challenge it: they saw a **429** on web.archive.org,
which is a rate limit rather than an outage, and that prompted the recount below.

The recount uses the **12 prefixes pulled on 2026-09-06** — 79,853 records, a different
day and a different query from the `z` probe — kept at
`data/cdx-index/raw-2025-partial-20260906/`.

| quarter | captures | 403 share | status 200 | gig-shaped 200 | distinct gigs |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2025Q1 | 21,751 | — | 3,626 | 2,720 | 1,002 |
| 2025Q2 | 14,356 | — | 2,055 | 1,347 | 610 |
| 2025Q3 | 18,998 | — | 2,230 | 1,338 | 970 |
| 2025Q4 | 12,601 | 1.0% | 2,239 | 1,231 | 940 |
| 2026Q1 | 9,734 | 18.1% | 1,180 | 799 | 563 |
| **2026Q2** | 1,204 | **89.3%** | 9 | 1 | **1** |
| **2026Q3** | 1,209 | **59.1%** | 152 | 62 | **50** |

**The mechanism is the wall, not abandonment.** The 403 share runs 1.0% -> 18.1% ->
**89.3%** -> 59.1% across the last four quarters. Wayback kept requesting fiverr.com
right through 2026Q3; it was refused. Capture volume also falls ~8x from 2026Q1, so the
right edge loses twice over, exactly as the 2025 analysis found.

**There is more in 2026Q3 than the `z` probe suggested.** 50 distinct gig pages captured
at status 200, in a pull covering **14.5%** of those 12 prefixes' pages (927 of 6,384).
Scaling by page coverage and by the 12 prefixes' share of the index gives an order of
**350-500 distinct gigs** archive-wide in 2026Q3. That is not nothing, and it is worth
finishing the refresh to collect.

**It still yields no matched pairs, which is the number that matters.** A bilateral needs
the same gig priced in two adjacent quarters. 2026Q2 holds **one** gig-shaped 200 in this
sample and 2026Q3 holds 50, so 2026Q2->Q3 pairs are ~0 archive-wide, and 2026Q1->Q2 the
same. The conclusion the first version reached survives its own arithmetic being wrong —
but it survives for a different reason, and at a different magnitude, than it claimed.

**2026Q3 is recovering, and that is new.** 403 share falls 89.3% -> 59.1% and status-200
captures rise 9 -> 152 between Q2 and Q3. Whether that is the wall easing or a change in
IA's crawl cadence cannot be told from the index alone, but it makes a *later* re-query
worth more than this one, and it is the first non-monotone quarter since the wall began.

## 2. Common Crawl: never had Fiverr, and still does not

Never previously considered by this project. CC publishes monthly indexes through
**August 2026**, exactly the window Wayback lacks — so it looked like the obvious
alternative. It is not:

- `CC-MAIN-2026-34` (Aug 2026), `url=fiverr.com/*`: **1 index block**. Of 200 sampled
  records, 128 are 403 and 72 are 301; **0 gig-shaped 200s**.
- Filtering to `status:200` returns **29 records, every one of them `robots.txt`**.
- `CC-MAIN-2024-33` and `CC-MAIN-2025-33` also return **1 block each** — so this is not
  the 2025 wall, CC never crawled Fiverr gigs at all (it honours the robots.txt it
  keeps re-fetching).

Common Crawl is closed as a source. Recorded so it is not re-tried.

## 3. It is a rate limit, so the refresh is paused rather than dead

The full 26-prefix refresh launched at 23:00 UTC and never got a page count: first
`TimeoutError`, then `Connection refused` from 207.241.237.3 (the host's only A record),
while `archive.org` itself answered 200 and the `z` pilot twenty minutes earlier had
succeeded.

From this host that was ambiguous between an outage and a block. **The operator resolved
it: web.archive.org served them a 429 for 2026 queries in a browser.** A 429 is a rate
limit — IA is up, and we are being throttled after yesterday's ~11 GB download and 499
rate-limit hits. It clears with time, so the refresh is **paused, not dead**, and the
right response is to wait and then pace far more gently rather than to retry now.

The run was stopped rather than left retrying, and the per-page checkpoints mean a resume
costs nothing; `z` sits intact at 48/96. Resume with:

    python3 code/01-download-cdx-index.py --from 20250101000000 --to 20260907000000 --out raw-2025

`MIN_INTERVAL` in that step is currently 0.75s globally. Given the throttle, raise it
before the next attempt.

## Conclusion

Wayback is throttling us, not empty — but on the numbers it can still only offer a few
hundred 2026Q3 gig captures archive-wide and **no matched pairs**, because 2026Q2 is all
but gone behind the wall. Common Crawl offers nothing at all. So the archives are worth
finishing for 2025 and for a count of 2026Q3, and they are not a route to a 2026 price
index.

The only surface that carries 2026 prices in usable quantity is a **live gig page**,
which step 80 measured as hard-blocked to scripted requests (15/15 HTTP 403, PerimeterX).
Steps 63 and 63b already established the route works if the pages can be fetched:

- **63A:** late-fetched pages reproduce an old quarter's price distribution — median
  realised value late minus early **+0 USD**, bootstrap CI [+0, +0]. No price selection.
- **63B:** but a live fetch reaches only **26-32%** of panel gigs, and selectively —
  43.8% live in the top listed-price quartile against 22.4% in the bottom, 55.0% in the
  top review-count quartile against 20.8% in the bottom.
- **63b reach:** a page fetched today shows orders at **median lag 2 months**, 74.7%
  within 3 months, 90.2% within 12 — so a fetch in 2026-09 lands squarely on the missing
  2026Q2-Q3 window, at ~13x fewer orders per page than a contemporaneous capture.

So the blocker is access, not method.
