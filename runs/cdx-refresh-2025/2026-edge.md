# Is there any 2026 Fiverr data left to collect?

**Run:** 2026-09-07, ~23:00-23:10 UTC. Three sources tested, cheaply, before committing
to any large pull. Two are exhausted; the third is the only one that reaches 2026Q2-Q3.

## 1. Wayback: 2026Q2-Q3 is empty, not thin

Prefix `z`, freshly pulled today over the full window `20250101 -> 20260907` (48 of 96
CDX pages, 1,465 records), is the first look this project has had at 2026Q2-Q3 — the
March 2026 index stops at 2026-03.

Status mix over the whole prefix: 200 539, `-` 391, 403 285, 301 158, 302 74, 404 17.

**21 captures across 2026Q2+Q3, one of them a 200.** The 2026Q1 -> 2026Q2 fall is 173 ->
11, an order of magnitude, and it is not the PerimeterX wall (403s do not rise, the
captures simply stop). Wayback appears to have stopped crawling fiverr.com after
2026Q1. Extrapolated over 26 prefixes this is a low-hundreds pool of records, near-zero
gig-shaped 200s, and — since a matched pair needs two visits — **zero usable pairs**.

The refresh remains worth finishing for **2025**, where 14 of 26 prefixes were never
pulled at all. It is not worth finishing for 2026.

## 2. Common Crawl: never had Fiverr, and still does not

Never previously considered by this project. CC publishes monthly indexes through
**August 2026**, exactly the window Wayback lacks — so it looked like the obvious
alternative. It is not:

- `CC-MAIN-2026-34` (Aug 2026), `url=fiverr.com/*`: **1 index block**. Of 200 sampled
  records, 128 are 403 and 72 are 301; **0 gig-shaped 200s**.
- Filtering to `status:200` returns **29 records, every one of them `robots.txt`**.
- `CC-MAIN-2024-33` and `CC-MAIN-2025-33` also return **1 block each** — so this is not
  the 2025 wall, CC never crawled Fiverr gigs at all (it honours the robots.txt it
  keeps re-fetching).

Common Crawl is closed as a source. Recorded so it is not re-tried.

## 3. web.archive.org refused connections mid-run

The full 26-prefix refresh launched at 23:00 UTC and never got a page count: first
`TimeoutError`, then `Connection refused` from 207.241.237.3 (the host's only A record).
`archive.org` itself answers 200, so this is Wayback-specific, and the `z` pilot 20
minutes earlier succeeded. Either an IA outage or an IP-level block following yesterday's
~11 GB download plus 499 rate-limit hits.

The run was **stopped rather than left retrying** — hammering a refusing host is futile
and impolite, and the per-page checkpoints mean a resume costs nothing. `z` sits intact
at 48/96. Resume with:

    python3 code/01-download-cdx-index.py --from 20250101000000 --to 20260907000000 --out raw-2025

## Conclusion

The archive route to 2026 is closed on both archives. The only remaining route to
2026Q2-Q3 prices is **live gig pages**, which step 80 measured as hard-blocked to
scripted requests (15/15 HTTP 403, PerimeterX). Steps 63 and 63b already established
that the route would work if the pages could be fetched:

- **63A:** late-fetched pages reproduce an old quarter's price distribution — median
  realised value late minus early **+0 USD**, bootstrap CI [+0, +0]. No price selection.
- **63B:** but a live fetch reaches only **32.1%** of panel gigs, and selectively —
  43.8% live in the top listed-price quartile against 22.4% in the bottom, 55.0% in the
  top review-count quartile against 20.8% in the bottom.
- **63b reach:** a page fetched today shows orders at **median lag 2 months**, 74.7%
  within 3 months, 90.2% within 12 — i.e. a fetch in 2026-09 lands squarely on the
  missing 2026Q2-Q3 window, at ~13x fewer orders per page than a contemporaneous capture.

So the blocker is access, not method.
